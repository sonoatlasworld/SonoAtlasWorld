Overview

A visual research tour

Ultrasound is an active sensing problem. A small movement of the probe changes the visible anatomy, and a wide sweep may revisit the same structures from a different acoustic window. A useful scanning environment must connect those observations to one persistent anatomy.

SonoAtlasWorld separates the anatomical state from the image generator. A 3D label volume supplies pose-dependent anatomy and visibility conditions; a causal video model learns ultrasound appearance and temporal continuity from clinical videos.

The study uses an 82,158-clip clinical ultrasound corpus with patient-disjoint splits, and 493 anatomical assets split into 453 training and 40 held-out volumes.

  • Anatomy as state. A fixed 3D asset anchors the scan, so different paths query the same underlying anatomy.

  • Probe actions as an interface. Translation and rotation update the probe pose, which determines the anatomical cross-section and acoustic visibility.

  • Causal observations. Generated history supplies temporal context, supporting interactive scanning and revisits over long rollouts.

Anatomy as State

A persistent CT-derived 3D label volume represents anatomy. Probe actions update the pose and restore skin contact. The sampler extracts anatomy and acoustic visibility at that pose, and the causal generator produces the next ultrasound block using the condition and generated history.

The anatomical asset remains fixed throughout the scan. It anchors revisits and different probe trajectories to the same underlying spatial structure.

The generator receives sampled 2D anatomy and visibility conditions rather than directly processing the full 3D volume. The anatomical asset and the observation history serve different roles.

From Scanning Experience to Policy Learning

The world model generates observations along offline expert trajectories. A goal-conditioned diffusion policy learns probe actions from those demonstrations, then interacts with the frozen world model on unseen anatomies.

Scale the training experience

Increasing the training set from 20 to 453 anatomical volumes expands demonstrations from 1,688 to 39,246 trajectories. The policy architecture and training budget are held fixed.

Measure closed-loop acquisition

Goal-plane success rises from 48.2% to 81.2% across the runs displayed in the paper. Each policy is frozen during evaluation; observations respond to its newly predicted actions.

Paper protocol: 112 episodes on 36 held-out cases per configuration, GI score ≥ 0.6 from frame 5 onward, with a phase-triggered fine-scan window. This is trajectory-level acquisition in simulation. Displayed small-data runs are selected individual seeds, not seed averages, and are sensitive to training-seed variability.

Research summary

Learning robotic ultrasound policies requires interactive environments for scalable data generation and repeatable evaluation, while exploration on real patients is limited by acquisition costs and safety constraints. Generative world models offer an alternative, but image history and probe actions alone do not ensure anatomical consistency across wide-range scans and changing acoustic windows. We introduce SonoAtlasWorld, an ultrasound world model built on anatomy as state. A persistent 3D anatomical asset extracted from computed tomography anchors each scan to a specific anatomy. Probe actions determine anatomical and acoustic visibility conditions for a causal ultrasound generator trained on clinical videos. Our three-stage pipeline combines anatomy-conditioned pretraining, causal conversion and long-rollout alignment. On 40 held-out 3D volumes, reconstruction-based evaluation measures anatomical action following beyond image similarity. Long-rollout alignment reduces brightness drift by 3.46× after 40 repeated scan cycles. SonoAtlasWorld also generates offline policy-training demonstrations and supports anatomically grounded closed-loop evaluation on unseen anatomies in simulation.

Scope: the experiments establish controllable generation, anatomical evaluation and policy learning within simulation. Physical robotic scanning, tissue deformation and transfer to real patients require further validation.
